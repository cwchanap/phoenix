# Phoenix placeholder audio

These WAV files are project-generated placeholder tones created for the HPA-599 MVP closeout. They contain no externally sourced recording or composition and require no third-party attribution. They are intentionally disposable if authored audio replaces them later.

## HPA-459 farming success cues

`farm-hoe.wav`, `farm-plant.wav`, `farm-water.wav`, and `farm-harvest.wav` are project-generated with the same throwaway Python stdlib recipe as the HPA-599 tones (`wave`/`math`/`random` only; mono 16-bit 22050 Hz, <0.5 s each). No synthesis script is committed; regenerate by re-running the recipe below and normalizing peak amplitude to 0.85.

| File | Length | Timbre | Recipe |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| `farm-hoe.wav` | 0.22 s | low dirt thud | 85 Hz sine, 45 ms decay, plus a 12 ms white-noise burst |
| `farm-plant.wav` | 0.18 s | droplet pluck | single sine sweeping 620→170 Hz (exp. pitch falloff τ=30 ms) with 50 ms decay |
| `farm-water.wav` | 0.35 s | noise swish | one-sample high-passed white noise under a single full-length sine-squared swell |
| `farm-harvest.wav` | 0.28 s | bright two-note pop | 780 Hz blip then a 1170 Hz blip entering at 60 ms (60 ms decay), plus 20 ms sparkle noise |

All four map one-to-one through `GameHud._sfx_for_code()` to the farming success codes (`SOIL_TILLED`, `CROP_PLANTED`, `CROP_WATERED`, `CROP_HARVESTED`) and play through the existing SFX player, so the Sound setting governs them automatically.

## HPA-440 river and rain ambience

`river-ambience.wav` is a 5-second loop of nearby moving water for the west river, and `rain-ambience.wav` is a 5-second loop of steady rain for weather playback. Both are mono PCM-16 at 22050 Hz, exactly 5.0 s / 110250 frames.

Provenance (per the repo audio convention, see CLAUDE.md): generated with ElevenLabs Sound Effects v2 (`eleven_text_to_sound_v2`) through the ElevenLabs MCP creative flow `ATS3d0Ki7wCgYSmjD2rL`, parameters `duration_seconds=5.0`, `loop=true`, `prompt_influence=0.7`; four takes per asset, selected by measurement — river take `jFveZfGFwAWKVz1nu7qo`, rain take `jo08JStJ0FWzI5fcrnpA`. Prompts (verbatim): river — "Gentle river water flowing over smooth stones. Steady low and mid frequency burbling water bed, soft rolling texture, continuous and even, no splashes, no birds, no wind, no dramatic events, no silence gaps. Calm ambient background loop."; rain — "Steady soft rainfall on a quiet homestead. Dense gentle broadband rain bed with warm low-mid body, continuous and even, no thunder, no wind gusts, no individual water drops, no dramatic events, no silence gaps. Calm ambient background loop."

Post-processing applied to the 44.1 kHz stereo masters (disposable tooling; recipe recorded here): downmix to 22050 Hz mono PCM-16; near-silence strip; river got a one-pole lowpass tilt at 1400 Hz to pull energy toward the low/mid water bed; rain got soft compression of sparse spectral peaks (knee 0.18 FS) so the bed could reach the mix level under the 0.30 peak guard; each loop was rebuilt with a 100 ms equal-power circular crossfade whose body starts 100 ms into the source, so the loop wrap joins adjacent source samples and is seamless by construction; finally each bed was scaled to the accepted mix RMS (river 0.0557, rain 0.0697 — the level HPA-402's headroom plan was reviewed against). Committed source peaks: river 0.248, rain 0.229 — both under the 0.30 guard. MCP generation is not byte-reproducible; the committed WAVs are the artifacts of record and rerunning a generation produces new takes, not these files.

Both sidecars import as full-clip Forward loops (`edit/loop_mode=2`, `edit/loop_begin=0`, `edit/loop_end=-1`) at the repository-default `compress/mode=2` (QOA); the PCM fallback was not needed.

Acceptance verdict (measurement-based imported-stream review — headless quantitative analysis of the QOA-decoded imported `AudioStreamWAV` samples, not a human listening session): each bed was analyzed as a six-period (≥ 30 s) concatenation of its imported buffer. All five loop-wrap transitions measure 343 (river) / 3155 (rain) — the 39.7th / 74.9th percentile of the concatenated signal's ordinary adjacent-sample deltas — and the largest delta within ±32 samples of any wrap (1658 river, 98.2nd percentile / 4454 rain, 86.7th percentile) stays below the global delta maximum (4907 / 16467, which occurs elsewhere in ordinary playback), so the imported streams loop without a seam-localized click or level jump and `compress/mode=2` needed no fallback. Per-period 100 ms RMS envelopes are identical across all six periods (cross-period drift 1.0000 — no 5-second-periodic pulse; the only level movement is the source's natural texture, 30 s envelope max/min 2.17 river / 1.66 rain). River energy is 39% in 90–900 Hz with its remainder as 900–2200 Hz texture (centroid ≈ 1.2 kHz, max/median core bin 4.4x — noise-like, no tonal spikes); rain is dominant across 700–11000 Hz with a restrained low-mid body (centroid ≈ 5.3 kHz, 3.9x — no thunder-like transients). Equal-gain mix vs the imported `farm-day-loop.wav`: unweighted RMS puts both beds below the music (river -25.1 / rain -23.1 vs music -19.7 dBFS imported, i.e. 3.4–5.4 dB down), while A-weighted loudness puts them above it (-25.2 / -23.3 vs -35.7 dBFS(A)) because the music bed concentrates its energy below 900 Hz (centroid ≈ 123 Hz) where A-weighting falls off — first-order file-level relations only; they do not establish perceived loudness or masking for a human listener. Against the farming one-shots the beds measure smaller in level: 0.85–0.88 peaks vs ≤ 0.26 and 7.0–11.7 dB hotter RMS for the cues, with crest ratios 5.6x (`farm-hoe.wav`) / 4.0x (`farm-harvest.wav`) vs 4.5x river / 3.8x rain. River and Rain are clearly distinguishable by spectrum (centroids ≈ 1.2 kHz vs ≈ 5.3 kHz). Residual risk: measurement is not listening — the final human 30-second listen of both beds under music at PR closeout remains the merge-time gate. Note the river take is measurably brighter than the original deterministic bed (39% vs 66% in 90–900 Hz); if the listen finds it too crisp, the fix is a new ElevenLabs take with a darker prompt or a lower tilt cutoff — not code.
